How you're protected.

Letting a crew into your home comes with real risks — most of which you never think about until something goes wrong. Here's every layer we put between you and those risks, and how to read the paperwork yourself.

Layer 1 · Insurance

Our certificate of insurance — and how to read it.

This is a real, current certificate. We circled the five things that matter to you, so you can read ours — or anyone's — with confidence.

  1. 1

    The date it was issued

    Every certificate shows the day it was created (top right). Always make sure you're looking at a recent one — not last year's.

  2. 2

    The expiration date

    Each policy is active only through this date. If a contractor's coverage has expired, you're not protected — so ask for a current certificate before work starts.

  3. 3

    General liability

    If we damage your home — a spill, a ladder through the drywall, a broken window — this pays to fix it, not you. Covered up to $1,000,000 per occurrence.

  4. 4

    Workers' compensation

    Here's the one most homeowners don't know: if a worker is hurt in your home and the company carries no workers' comp, you can be held liable. This covers our people, so you never are.

  5. 5

    The certificate holder

    This is where the certificate holder's name and address go — on your job, that would be you. We blurred our client's here for their privacy. And if your HOA (or you) want a certificate naming you, just ask — we'll send one.

Before you hire anyone

How to protect yourself with any contractor.

Whether you hire us or not, here's what to ask everyone who wants to work in your home:

  • 01Ask for a current certificate of insurance — and check the issue and expiration dates.
  • 02Confirm they carry both general liability and workers' compensation.
  • 03Verify their state contractor registration number on the public registry.
  • 04Get the warranty in writing before any work begins.

If a contractor hesitates on any of these — that's your answer.
